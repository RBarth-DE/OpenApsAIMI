### The enum ordinal, and why the fork accepts it

The Android notification id is the enum ordinal, so inserting `BOLUS_CANCELLED` in the middle of the
enum moves every entry after it by one - `LIBRE3_DIR_ACCESS_LOST` and the rest of the fork's ids
included. Nothing reads an ordinal back: `fromOrdinal` has no caller outside its own declaration, so
there is no stored value to decode wrongly. The only effect is on an in-place upgrade, where a card
that is on the screen at that moment keeps its old id and is not replaced or dismissed by the new
code until the user swipes it away.

Upstream put `PLUGIN_START_FAILED` at the end for exactly this reason, and wrote the reason into the
enum. The fork keeps upstream's placement for `BOLUS_CANCELLED` rather than moving it to the end:
the shift costs one stale card once, and re-ordering the enum against upstream would conflict again
on every future merge.
